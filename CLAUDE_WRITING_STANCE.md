# Report what happened, not what you concluded about it

This file exists because a catalog of banned phrases kept failing. Jake would
object to "earned its keep," the phrase would go on a list, and three days
later the same impulse produced "earned its place," which the list did not
contain. The list records words. The impulse makes new ones.

So this file names the impulse.

## The move

Every one of those phrases does the same thing. I have a fact. Instead of
writing the fact, I write my verdict on the fact.

| What I wrote | The fact I had |
|---|---|
| the pattern earned its place | it found three uses of the figure in the note |
| the explanation landed | he could restate it without the file open |
| that is the price of the largest breakdown point | eight of the ten values reach the answer only through their rank |
| worth noticing, the median moves before it breaks | the median moves from 42.5 to 61.5 before it breaks |
| the guard passes | the note is still a draft, so no page from it was published |
| a load-bearing assumption | the variance calculation in Section 4 depends on it |
| we handed the job to an optimizer | I suggested we use an optimizer |
| the mean fell out of it | the formula arising from the criterion gave us the mean |
| we swept a grid, saw a curve that falls to one low point and rises again | `rbind(30:50, sum_sq_diffs[30:50])`, printing the scores from 3500 down to 2108 and back up to 2780 |
| that's not just inconvenient --- it throws away the very thing we said we wanted to measure | differences of 10 and -10 add to 0, and $10^2 + (-10)^2 = 200$ against $1^2 + (-1)^2 = 2$ |

The right-hand column is shorter in meaning and often shorter in words. The
left-hand column tells the reader what to think about material I did not give
them.

The last four rows are Jake's own edits, made on 2026-08-30 to a class note
Claude had drafted. Each replaces a description of the work with the work: an
actor who did the suggesting, the criterion that produced the mean, the
printed scores instead of a sentence about their shape, and, where the draft
had asserted that squaring fixes the cancellation, the two sums a reader can
compare.

## Why it happens

I am writing from inside my own experience of the work. I know the pattern was
useful, that the explanation succeeded, that the check ran. Those are facts
about my afternoon. The phrase reports my afternoon and leaves the reader with
nothing, because the reader was not there.

The vividness is what makes it hard to catch. "Earned its place" feels like
good writing. It is warm, confident, and it moves the sentence along. That
feeling is the tell: I reached for a figure because the plain statement felt
thin, and the plain statement felt thin because I had not finished it.

## Why it is worse than filler

Jake reads to decide. A verdict gives him nothing to decide with. And handing
a colleague a conclusion about evidence he can weigh himself is condescension,
whatever the tone.

## The rule, at drafting time

Write what happened. Do not write what you concluded about what happened. The
reader supplies the verdict; you supply the material.

Three questions, and they run while drafting rather than after:

1. Does this phrase characterize something I just said? Delete it. Nothing
   will be lost, because a characterization adds no information to the thing
   it characterizes.
2. Does this word name something the reader cannot see --- a script, a check,
   a step in my process, a file I read? Replace it with what it did.
3. Am I reaching for a vivid phrase? Then the plain statement felt thin. Go
   back and finish the plain statement instead of decorating it.

## Two things this rule does not ban

A **forecast** tells the reader where to look and carries information: "we
prove this in Section 4." Keep it.

A **relation to an expectation the reader already holds** also carries
information: "contrary to Fairfield and Charman's prediction." Keep that too,
provided the expectation was stated.

Both survive the deletion test in question 1: strike them and a claim
disappears with them. A verdict does not.

## The catalog's place

`skills/style-audit/SKILL.md` section 6 lists instances someone has already
caught. It is an audit aid, useful for finding the specific words that have
gone wrong before, and it is read after drafting, never during. It is not the
rule. This file is the rule, and it covers phrases nobody has written yet.

When a new offender turns up, the first question is not "what should I add to
the catalog?" It is "which fact did I have, and why did I write a verdict
instead?" If the answer is the one above, the catalog does not need a new row.
